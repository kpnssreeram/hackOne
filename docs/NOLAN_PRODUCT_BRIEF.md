# Nolan product brief

## The promise

Nolan turns one spoken or written idea into a complete three-episode audio story
that a first-time creator can understand without learning production software.

Each episode targets about 90 seconds. Nolan shows the entire arc first, then
only spends audio or image credits after the creator confirms the specific
episode they want made.

## Creator path

1. Tell Nolan the idea in the language you naturally speak or write.
2. Confirm the people, emotion, and details that must stay.
3. Choose a story direction based on tone and what actually happens, not
   technical categories.
4. See the complete three-part promise: beginning, escalation, and ending.
5. Draft Episode 1. Confirm it before audio is rendered.
6. Listen and give three plain-language notes:
   - What should stay?
   - What should change in this episode?
   - Where should the next episode go?
7. Approve it to carry its continuity into the next episode. Repeat through
   Episode 3, which ends the story rather than creating a forced cliffhanger.

## Voice and media

- A creator can assign their own consented voice sample to the narrator or any
  listed character.
- If a voice-cloning provider does not grant cloning access, Nolan says so
  plainly and continues with the selected stock voice. It never pretends a
  clone was made.
- Hindi is rendered through the dependable multilingual path. Telugu and other
  scripts outside that path use the expressive multilingual path so the spoken
  language and the written emotion stay aligned.
- A photo stays a photo, and a video clip stays a real clip in the visual edit.
  A place reference can inform the setting of missing fictional scenes; it is
  never treated as a request to generate a real person.
- Nolan can make a story cover and a low-credit visual edit plan. Full
  generative video is always a separate, explicit creator choice—not an
  automatic surprise charge.

## Credit policy

- One story-plan call creates all three episode outlines.
- One script and one quality check are created for the selected episode only.
- Audio, cover images, and visual planning wait for creator confirmation.
- A revision re-renders only the episode that changed.
- Uploaded creator media is reused directly instead of regenerated.

## Story quality contract

Nolan checks every episode against the Pocket FM story rules:

1. An audible hook arrives quickly.
2. The story works without visuals.
3. Pressure rises.
4. Characters make choices that fit their motivation.
5. Every sound cue changes place, pressure, or story information.
6. Narration is used only when dialogue or sound cannot do the job; silence is
   a valid sound choice.
7. Episodes 1 and 2 end on a consequential unanswered turn. Episode 3 earns
   an ending payoff.
8. The creator's protected story details remain intact.

## Nolan's build prompt

> Build the smallest clear path to a moving finished story. Start with the
> creator's language, voice, and protected story details. Show the whole arc,
> but create costly media only after a clear confirmation. Let writers,
> directors, sound, and visual work report real completed work in simple
> language—never raw model JSON or fake agent chatter. Preserve continuity,
> make feedback local to the episode it changes, and let Episode 3 resolve the
> promise. Prioritize Hindi and Telugu delivery, useful sound, natural use of
> creator media, and a UI a nontechnical creator can operate in one pass.

## Validation checklist

- Three outlines are exactly Episodes 1, 2, and 3.
- Drafting never renders audio before confirmation.
- A later episode receives the approved continuity and direction from the
  earlier episode.
- Revising an earlier episode makes only unapproved later work stale.
- Telugu delivery uses the expressive multilingual model; Hindi keeps its
  native text and emotional direction.
- Uploaded media requires consent, uses safe file types, and is represented in
  the visual plan.
- Final duration is recorded so the creator can tighten or extend an episode
  rather than silently spending credits again.
